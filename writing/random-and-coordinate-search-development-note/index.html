Random, coordinate, and live LLM search at a fixed budget

A bounded synthetic controller-search study, a promising frozen candidate, and the limits of one live search at a 20-evaluation budget.

2026-10-24 · Updated 2026-10-24

What does a small baseline and proposer comparison tell us?

Before asking whether an AI proposer improves a controller, we need to understand what simpler searches achieve under the same evaluation budget, and then test an isolated language model under identical constraints. This note examines both questions on a deliberately small synthetic task:

  1. Can a fixed-rule coordinate search (B2) find a lower development cost than the average uniform-random search (B1) after 20 candidate evaluations?
  2. With the same 20-evaluation budget, can an LLM proposer that receives only development-test information and cannot use tools outperform both B1 and B2 without unsafe rollouts? The live method is labeled A-live-proposer (recorded model label gemini-4-argon-high, search seed 2000).
  3. Do the selected controller parameters generalize to an untouched, one-shot holdout split (seeds 1000..1029, 90 rollouts per candidate) on the registered holdout cases?

On the baseline development comparison, coordinate search (0.094934) finishes slightly ahead of the random-search mean (0.095519), though random search wins five of the ten individual comparisons, has a slightly better median (0.094902), and leads at intermediate budgets (5, 10, and 15 evaluations). When the blinded live proposer (A-live-proposer, seed 2000) is evaluated under the same 20-candidate budget, it reaches 0.092980 by evaluation 5 and finishes at 0.092429 (slot 19), beating B2 (-0.002504), the B1 mean (-0.003089), B1 seed 2000 (-0.000965), and all 10 B1 streams.

No holdout evaluation was permitted or accessed during candidate proposal, tuning, or winner selection. The frozen development winners were subsequently evaluated on the preregistered one-shot holdout split (seeds 1000..1029, 90 rollouts per distinct candidate). Their aggregate costs were lower on these holdout cases; this is a finite-sample observation, not proof that development selection avoided overfitting. On holdout, A-live-proposer finishes at 0.092063, maintaining its cost advantage over B1 (mean 0.094983, best seed 0.093045) and B2 (0.094360), supporting the preregistered exploratory holdout criterion for search seed 2000 (10,000 crossed bootstrap replicates, seed 4000: vs B1 crossed 97.5% CI [-0.004664, -0.001738]; vs B2 97.5% CI [-0.002525, -0.002069]). The ten-search confirmatory comparison remains inconclusive because searches with seeds 2001–2009 were not run.

The task and comparison

The frozen specification defines a scalar plant with nominal, disturbance-shift, and saturation-limited regimes. Each simulated trial, or rollout, lasts 200 steps and follows the same piecewise-constant target schedule. The controller has proportional, integral, and derivative gains plus an option to enable or disable anti-windup. Gains are constrained to kp in [0, 2] and ki/kd in [0, 0.5], on a six-decimal grid. The simulator and evaluator own the dynamics and scoring; a proposal cannot supply executable code.

Cost is the average of squared tracking error, plus 0.01 times control effort, plus 0.05 times squared changes in control, plus 10 times the unsafe-state fraction. The objective uses pre-update states. Terminal state is excluded from cost but included in maximum-state and terminal-safety diagnostics. Each candidate is scored on development seeds 0–9 in all three regimes, for 30 rollouts. Those same cases are reused throughout selection and comparison.

Five methods are recorded across the baseline study and live proposer study:

Method Procedure Evaluations per run
B0 Fixed, untuned reference: kp=0.5, ki=0.05, kd=0, anti-windup enabled 1
B1 Registered uniform sampling over the permitted controller family (10 seeds: 2000–2009) 20
B2 Coordinate search that tests nearby settings within the allowed bounds, chooses the best improvement in each sweep, and skips duplicates 20
Mock A fixed script of controller proposals (A-mock), used to check the research software 20
A-live LLM proposer with no access to holdout results (recorded model label: gemini-4-argon-high; A-live-proposer, seed 2000) using development-only history 20

Every search counts the reference controller as its first evaluation. Up to 60 proposal attempts are allowed; rejected duplicates consume attempts but do not receive another evaluation. The winner is the lowest-cost successful candidate, with the earliest candidate winning an exact tie. B0 is a reference, not a budget-matched search.

The baseline comparison registration and live proposer registration specified the directional questions and descriptive analyses before execution. The live proposer ran in a separate conversation under a protocol that prohibited tool use. The saved audit reports zero tool calls; this review did not independently inspect the external platform configuration. The proposer received a structured description of the task, controller limits, scoring rules, and previous development-test results. It had no access to holdout results.

B2 and the mock do not vary with the search seed; their ten executions each reproduced exactly the same candidates, scores, and simulated trajectories, so the analysis counts one deterministic path for B2 and one for the mock. A-live-proposer was executed once on the preregistered primary repetition (seed 2000) and checked offline by feeding its saved proposals back into the evaluator.

Between B1 and B2, B1's mean best cost so far is lower at 5, 10, and 15 evaluations (0.100814 vs 0.121489 at slot 5), while B2 only catches up near the end of its fourth coordinate sweep (selecting slot 19, kp=1.5, ki=0.3, kd=0, anti_windup=true). By contrast, A-live-proposer combines analytical reasoning about the plant dynamics (a=0.9, b=0.2 and a=0.95, b=0.1) with iterative score feedback:

  • Slots 2–7 (coarse regime identification): Starting from the reference controller (kp=0.5, ki=0.05, kd=0.0, cost 0.172740), it tests analytical critical damping (kp=1.2, ki=0.2, kd=0.0 -> 0.099146 at slot 2), sees that higher gains improve tracking (kp=1.8, ki=0.32, kd=0.0 -> 0.093799 at slot 3; kp=1.85, ki=0.45, kd=0.0 -> 0.092980 at slot 5), and reaches the upper proportional-integral corner (kp=2.0, ki=0.5, kd=0.0 -> 0.092447 at slot 7), before testing positive kd (0.01, 0.002) and interior ki (0.485) across slots 8–10. Those particular proposals did not improve the best cost so far; they do not rule out other gains.
  • Slots 11–20 (bisecting a conditional-integration discontinuity): Probing interior kp values (1.96 -> 0.092445 at slot 11; 1.978 -> 0.092431 at slot 12), it predicts a smooth quadratic minimum at kp=1.97935 (slot 13) but observes an upward cost step to 0.092471. Its recorded rationale hypothesizes a switch in conditional integration near a saturation threshold, then bisects [1.978, 1.97935] down to [1.978644, 1.978666], selecting slot 19 (kp=1.978644, ki=0.5, kd=0.0, anti_windup=true, cost 0.092429429500).

Where the cost and constraints appear

Across all methods, the saturation-limited regime (a=0.95, b=0.1, u_max=0.35) dominates total cost because its noiseless steady-state ceiling during the +1 target segment is 0.7. Relative to B2 (nominal=0.042638, disturbance-shift=0.054183, saturation-limited=0.187979) and the B1 mean (nominal=0.042504, disturbance-shift=0.054426, saturation-limited=0.189627), A-live-proposer lowers cost in all three regimes simultaneously (nominal=0.039931, disturbance-shift=0.049875, saturation-limited=0.187482), with the largest absolute reductions in disturbance-shift (-0.004308 vs B2) and nominal (-0.002707 vs B2) while using a higher integral gain (ki=0.5). These aggregate comparisons do not isolate the cause of the improvement.

Tracking error dominates the weighted objective across all five methods (0.087431 of 0.092429 for A-live-proposer, 0.090490 of 0.094934 for B2), while weighted effort (0.002468) and slew (0.002530) remain small but consequential for fine-grained ranking.

No numerical failures, unknown diagnostics, unsafe pre-update states, or unsafe terminals were recorded across any baseline or live run. Across the 600 development rollouts of A-live-proposer, maximum absolute state was 1.614522 (1.259079 for its selected winner), well below the 2.5 safety threshold. Its selected winner saturated on 1,116 / 6,000 steps (18.60%), within the range of the ten B1 winners (15.03%–26.43%) and slightly above B2 (16.93%). Saturation is a separate actuator-constraint diagnostic, not a safety violation.

Testing outcome feedback and conditional anti-windup

To compare one search with outcome feedback against one without it, and examine conditional anti-windup, the project executed the preregistered ablation suite (saved numeric results) on the development split (search seed 2000, with no holdout evaluations):

  1. Proposer outcome feedback ablation (A-live-no-history vs. A-live-proposer): A separate model conversation, using the same recorded model label, received the same instructions and simulator specification across 20 evaluations. Each request omitted previous test outcomes and information about the best controller found so far. The recorded no-feedback rationales emphasize linear pole-placement calculations around critical damping (z approximately 0.80), sampling 19 distinct candidates clustered in kp in [1.05, 1.35], ki in [0.15, 0.22], kd in [0.0, 0.03] and selecting slot 15 (kp=1.32, ki=0.21, kd=0.015, anti_windup=true) with mean cost 0.097565345. This no-feedback search beats B0-fixed (0.172740) and A-mock (0.098299), but loses to B2-coordinate-search (0.094934, -0.002632) and the B1-uniform-random mean (0.095519, -0.002046). The no-feedback run’s development cost is 0.005135916 higher than the history-aware run’s (0.097565 versus 0.092429). One run per condition cannot establish that feedback is necessary or isolate its average causal effect from model sampling variation. The no-history condition removes outcome feedback; it does not start a new model conversation for every proposal.
  2. Controller conditional anti-windup ablation (disabled versus enabled): The registered no-anti-windup variants have higher development costs in all five selected comparisons: B0 (0.172740 -> 0.175564, +0.002824, 100% in saturation-limited), B1 seed 2000 (0.093394 -> 0.102579, +0.009185), B2 seed 2000 (0.094934 -> 0.098565, +0.003631), A-live trajectory best (0.092429 -> 0.098759 at slot 4, +0.006329), and the A-live slot 19 winner evaluated with anti-windup disabled (0.092429 -> 0.114023, +0.021593, driven by a +0.060461 surge in saturation-limited cost). The exact-gain live pair isolates the effect of enabling anti-windup and shows its benefit for that controller. B1/B2 rerun their registered searches with anti-windup disabled; the live counterfactual re-scores the saved proposal sequence rather than running a new live search. Those selected-winner contrasts also change gains and do not isolate the switch alone.

Preregistered uncertainty estimates and conclusions

The registered bootstrap uses 10,000 replicates (random seed 4000, using the registered random-number procedure), resampling whole seed blocks across the three scenarios. P1 also resamples B1 search repetitions; P2 resamples seed blocks only. Both condition on the single frozen live candidate, so neither captures live-search variability:

  • Primary comparison P1 (A-live vs. B1 10-rep crossed): Mean difference is -0.002920 (relative improvement +3.07%), with 97.5% Bonferroni CI [-0.004664, -0.001738] (95% CI [-0.004399, -0.001837]), winning 30/30 holdout seed blocks and 10/10 repetition slots.
  • Primary comparison P2 (A-live vs. B2 deterministic): Mean difference is -0.002298 (relative improvement +2.43%), with 97.5% Bonferroni CI [-0.002525, -0.002069], winning 30/30 holdout seed blocks.
  • Registered conclusions: The results support the predicted improvement for the one live-selected controller. The full confirmatory comparison requires ten independent live searches under sections 6–7 of the specification. Searches with seeds 2001–2009 were not run, so that comparison remains inconclusive.
  • Descriptive ablations on holdout: A-live (2000) vs. A-live-no-history (2000) has holdout difference -0.005248 (97.5% CI [-0.005664, -0.004863]). For the five registered no-anti-windup contrasts, all 5 pairs show strictly positive holdout cost degradation without anti-windup (all nominal 97.5% intervals are above zero, reaching +0.021453 for exact gains). These secondary intervals are not familywise-adjusted across the five ablations and do not establish that feedback or anti-windup is universally necessary.

Mechanism and trajectory analysis

These are preregistered examples from one noise seed, not estimates of typical rise time, settling time or overshoot. The nominal live trajectory exceeds the +1 target after the first step, so it does not support a “no overshoot” claim. No settling tolerance was registered; noisy error values cannot substantiate “zero error within eight steps.”

The saturation figure shows the retained integrator state. Conditional integration blocks updates when the tentative action is outside the actuator limit and the current error would push it further into saturation; it can still integrate while unwinding. Integral clipping also applies to all variants. The saved examples illustrate this rule without proving that it is the sole reason for the search outcome.

Evidence, provenance and limits

The downloadable numeric evidence includes frozen controller parameters, method summaries, primary comparisons and the incomplete confirmatory comparison. The 1,800 saved holdout rows expose per-seed objective components and safety diagnostics. They are published records, not a new evaluation.

The benchmark configuration, original specification, baseline registration, live-proposer registration, ablation registration and holdout registration preserve the historical rules. Statements of pending work in those registrations describe their pre-execution status.

Plot data are available for the development budget curves, holdout costs, bootstrap comparisons, anti-windup comparisons and example trajectories. Figure exports retain their reviewed numeric data; plot CSVs use display precision, while the numeric evidence and rollout records retain full precision.

Two fresh local copies of the project reproduced the scripted run and both searches using saved model responses. The simulated trajectories matched for every seed, and resuming an already completed run performed no additional simulation. These checks ran using Node 22.19.0, npm 10.9.3, CPython 3.13.1 and R 4.4.3 on one macOS host. All 13 diagnostic figures reproduced byte-for-byte. The retained records separate historical finish timestamps from unknown starts; no missing runtime was reconstructed. These are local software checks: replaying saved responses does not regenerate a model's proposals or estimate its sampling variability. Research source code, raw conversations and checkpoints are not included in this website release, so it is an inspectable results package rather than a complete public reproduction archive.

Key scientific limitations:

  1. Inspected holdout split: Seeds 1000..1029 count as inspected. Any future hyperparameter or controller tuning must be designated exploratory; rigorous confirmation would require an untouched holdout split under a future benchmark version.
  2. Single live search repetition (seed 2000): A-live-proposer was executed once. The crossed bootstrap against B1 resamples B1 repetitions but repeats the single A-live candidate, thereby omitting A-live's own search-to-search variance. This evidence supports the effectiveness of the discovered controller, not multi-seed stability of the proposer method in general.
  3. Budget and provenance: Twenty candidate evaluations match simulator budgets, not total compute, model calls or elapsed time. Saved records identify the execution platform, model label and version of the proposer instructions; the historical record reports 19 calls and approximately 17,959 tokens for the history-aware run, with $0 marginal API spend under an included subscription. This review verifies local records, not provider identity or billing.
  4. Synthetic benchmark boundary: Performance is synthetic and bounded by the scalar linear plant family.

The next useful experiment would register multiple independent proposer searches and a new untouched evaluation split. It would test how often the result recurs, before asking whether a more elaborate research procedure helps.
